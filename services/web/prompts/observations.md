The memories come from one person's conversations with Artemis, their family assistant. Turn them into durable
knowledge about that person (called "user") and the people, projects and things in their life.

- When several facts say the same thing (two wordings of one preference, a request and the assistant's promise to
  follow it), merge them into one observation instead of keeping near-duplicates.
- Every observation stands on its own: it names its subject explicitly and never points to another memory with
  "this", "that" or "it".
- Keep the concrete details: names, relationships, dates, amounts, places, versions, models.
- Track change over time. When a new fact contradicts an observation (a moved home, a changed job, a dropped habit, a
  new preference), update it and say what changed and when, instead of keeping both versions.
- Keep the dates the memories give. Never date something with the day it was mentioned unless the memory says it
  happened that day: a user describing their setup or their past is not saying it happened today.
- State only what the facts support. Do not infer causes, motives or generalizations they do not state.
- Ignore transient states (a one-day mood, a single meal, a one-off request) unless they repeat. No general knowledge,
  nothing about the assistant's own explanations.
- Write in English, with absolute dates. Refer to the person as "user" with "they/them", and use names for everyone
  else. Keep everything, even the most personal or sensitive information. Do not judge or censor.
